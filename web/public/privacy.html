Crownfall: privacy

Last updated 9 November 2026

Crownfall is a game on NEAR testnet, built for the NEAR HACKATHON. This page says what it does with your data when you sign in. The code is public at github.com/neromtoobad/crownfall.

Signing in with Google

Google sends the game a signed token. The game checks it with Google and reads two things from it: your Google account's id and your first name. From the id it derives a key for your NEAR testnet account; from the first name it makes the account's name (for example ada-x7k2.vael.testnet). It does not keep your email, your photo or the token, and it has no database of players.

Signing in with a name

Your browser makes its own key and sends only the public half. The game makes the account and never sees the private key.

What is public

Your NEAR testnet account name and everything it does in the game (coins bought, rounds played, bets) are on a public blockchain, like any NEAR account. The name you give your lord and your house stay in your browser.

What stays in your browser

Your account's key, your seat, your practice purse and your progress are kept in your browser's local storage. Signing out keeps the key so you can come back; clearing your browser's data removes it.

No tracking

No analytics, no ads, no cookies of our own. The site is hosted on Vercel, which keeps standard server logs.

Contact

Questions or a request to remove anything: open an issue on the GitHub repository.
